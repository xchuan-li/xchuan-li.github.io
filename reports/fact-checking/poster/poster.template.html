Taking a Language Model’s Word for It?

How people check answers from an AI chatbot and from an encyclopedia

Take-home. People who could check with an AI chatbot first did not double-check less; descriptively, they double-checked more. Neither group difference was reliable (N = {{N}}).

Introduction

Language models now answer many everyday questions. Whether people let such an answer settle a question shapes how information passes between AI systems and humans.

ChatGPT is discussed as an epistemic authority: a source whose answer counts as a reason to believe (Cooper et al., 2024; Hauswald, 2025). Relying on it is a form of cognitive offloading (Shanmugasundaram & Tamilarasu, 2023).

Does checking with an AI chatbot first, rather than an encyclopedia, change how often people check their answers, and how often they check again elsewhere?

H1With ChatGPT first, people check more answers in round 1. H2With ChatGPT first, people check fewer answers with the second source.

Method

Design
Between subjects; first tool assigned at random: Wikipedia first or ChatGPT first.
Sample
{{N_TOTAL}} took part, {{N_DECLINED}} declined consent; {{N}} analysed ({{NW}} and {{NG}}). Convenience sample.
Task
Ten two-option general-knowledge questions (What is the coloured portion of the eye called? Iris / Pupil). Each tool’s answer appeared as a fixed screenshot.
Measures
Answers checked in round 1 and in round 2 (each 0–10); certainty; accuracy.
Analysis
Welch’s t-tests, Cohen’s d, Pearson correlations. Unanswered check prompts count as not checked.

Does doubt drive double-checking?

If it does, round-2 checks should rise as accuracy and certainty fall.

Conclusions

  • An AI-first check did not reduce double-checking; descriptively, more followed.
  • If this holds in larger samples, AI answers prompt further checking rather than settle the question.
  • Double-checking did not follow doubt; one-click checks may explain how often people checked.

Limitations and next steps

Small, unbalanced sample; easy items; cost-free checks. Next: a larger sample, harder items, checks that cost time, and item-level models of certainty and trust in each tool.
