Computational cognitive science

How is language learned, and how does it shape understanding and thought?

I study how people learn from linguistic experience and how that experience shapes comprehension and reasoning. Alongside behavioural studies, I use computational models to quantify regularities in linguistic input and test hypotheses about human cognition. In the long run, I want to know what happens to a person’s meaning when an artificial system takes part in their thinking and expression.

I am a master’s student in Human and Artificial Intelligence at the University of Technology Nuremberg, with a background in philosophy. My master’s thesis, on modal language in preschool children, is run within a PhD project at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences in Leipzig; my project on moral framing and dilemma choices in language models was supervised at the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI).

    When moral wording changes a model’s choice, what changes: the weighing of outcomes, a tendency to answer with action, or some other process? And how far can internal interventions tell these apart?
