Research Program

Question 1

How is language learned, and how does it shape understanding and thought?

1aLearning

What information does language contain that lets a learner form meanings, concepts and the ability to reason?

Work so far: The direction of disagreement, StemmaLM, Modal language in preschool children.

1bShaping

How does meeting and using a particular structure of language change understanding, reasoning and habits of thought?

Work so far: Moral framing and dilemma choices in language models.

1cConnecting

When an expression passes through an artificial model or a neural decoding system, which meanings are preserved, and which are filled in or changed?

Open

Question 2

When artificial systems take part in human cognition and expression, how can a person’s meaning be understood, supported and preserved?

Question 2 asks the three parts of Question 1 again, for the case where a machine is the learner, the source of the language, or the channel.

2aLearning

How do machines obtain, from language, structure that can be used for understanding and reasoning?

Work so far: The direction of disagreement, StemmaLM.

2bShaping

How does long exposure to machine-generated language change people’s understanding and habits of thought?

Work so far: Taking a language model’s word for it?.

2cConnecting

When a machine helps a person express something, how can we tell whether it kept the original meaning or introduced a new one?

People and models

Human and model studies can inform each other without assuming that they use the same mechanisms. Similar answers can arise from different processes, and an intervention in a model does not establish a mechanism in people. What carries over between the two needs its own evidence.

How I came to these questions

My undergraduate studies in philosophy drew me to logic: a way to make the structure of an argument explicit and examine what follows from it. In my thesis, I studied Leibniz’s logical system and his proposal for a universal symbolic language. This raised a question that continues to interest me: how do formal descriptions of meaning relate to the ways people actually understand and reason?

Learning languages gave that question an everyday setting. I found myself paying attention to how sentence structure contributes to meaning, and how different languages can express similar ideas through different structures. This led me to ask when differences in linguistic form affect understanding and thought. Alongside philosophy, I studied psychology and took part in a project on children’s learning difficulties. My master’s studies in Human and Artificial Intelligence have brought these interests into experimental and computational work.
