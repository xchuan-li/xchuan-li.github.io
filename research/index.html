Research & Projects

Questions from linguistics and cognitive science, turned into controlled experiments on language models: what speakers’ disagreement about word boundaries tells a learner that sees only text, how moral framing changes a model’s dilemma choices, and, on the human side, how children come to tell possibility from necessity. Course projects and earlier studies with people sit alongside them.

Research

Questions from linguistics and cognitive science: the human evidence they start from, and controlled experiments on language models, behaviour first, then inside the model.

  1. Vagueness · controlled training

    Project · independent · preprint 2026

    The direction of disagreement

    Speakers disagree about where tall ends. For a learner that sees only text, is that disagreement noise, or evidence about the scale underneath?

    LingBuzz·DOI

  2. Mechanistic interpretability

    Project · 2026 · supervised at the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI) · interactive results

    Moral framing and dilemma choices in language models

    When moral wording changes a model’s choice, what changes: the weighing of outcomes, a tendency to answer with action, or some other process? And how far can internal interventions tell these apart?

  3. Modality · human side

    Project · master’s thesis · in planning, no data yet

    Modal language in preschool children

    How do preschool children come to tell possibility from necessity? The study lays the groundwork for later work on modal language in language models.

Course projects

  1. Model training · image CNN

    Project · team course project 2026 · report 2026

    Training choices in a small CNN for street-view country classification

    Training choices alone took a fixed 1.69M-parameter network from 0.62 to 0.82 validation accuracy; the best dropout rate depended on training length.

    PDF

  2. EEG experiment

    Project · course study, Winter 2026–27 · preregistration completed

    Immediate repetition and familiarity-like EEG representations of unfamiliar faces

    A preregistered representational similarity analysis of open EEG data: does seeing an unfamiliar face again move its EEG pattern toward the pattern for famous faces?

Earlier research experience

An experiment with people from a master’s course, and a reading assessment from an undergraduate internship.

  1. Behavioural experiment

    Course experiment · Winter 2025–26 · report and poster

    Taking a language model’s word for it?

    Participants answered ten general-knowledge questions and could check each answer, first with ChatGPT or Wikipedia (assigned at random), then with the other source. Starting with ChatGPT did not reduce double-checking (N = 47). Designed in a group of three and run by the whole class; I did the analysis and wrote the report and poster.

    PDF·Poster

  2. Child reading assessment

    Internship · September–December 2021

    Reading difficulties in primary-school children

    How does presenting a Chinese character alone or within a word change access to its pronunciation? A project report on reading assessment, with qualitative observations and character–word examples.

    Poster
